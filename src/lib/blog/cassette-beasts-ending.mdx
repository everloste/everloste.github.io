This post contains major spoilers. Read if you've finished the game, or won't play it yourself, or you're just like that, I don't care.

Bleh. I really wanted to love Cassette Beasts, I really did. And it was nice. But the ending? What.. happened?

I usually avoid making posts on media and what I think of them, like opinion pieces and reviews, because I'm a creative myself and, well, I want to avoid it coming across as hate or anything akin to burning bridges. So obviously, I do not mean this post like that. I liked Cassette Beasts! But, in my mind, the ending wasn't very good, and I wanted to explore why I think that is, because not often do I like a game enough for an ending to disappoint me this much.

In the slightest chance anyone from the Cassette Beasts team or someone related reads this (which, yes, I realise is *astronomically* unlikely, but I have come to realise that I am a creator whose works have been downloaded at least two times, so I probably get the tiniest bit more traffic than if that wasn't the case) please do take this as coming from someone with whom the game resonated a lot, and did like it a lot.

### The line-arc

From the very beginning the game establishes, somewhat firmly, that the player, and other characters on New Wirral, are stuck there, for better or worse. It also establishes that the player, and the other relevant characters, do want to find a way out, even if they deem it impossible at first.

Cue the game. You as a player get the option to build yourself relationships with the deuteragonists, as well as make one of them your romantic partner. My girlfriend and I went with Kayleigh, so we didn't get to learn as much about the other characters, but still. (We played local co-op.)

Then, the ending. You find the magic exit, 3D Bill Cipher follows you, wants to destroy every world, you stop him, fantastic. As clichéd as it might appear, to me, this is perfectly fine, as there's realistically next to no wiggle room in how it could go.

So now before you stands the Oxenfree-esque universe gate, (seemingly) giving you, and every other character a choice - **do you go back to your old world, or decide to stay in this one?** Or perhaps, do you just extend your stay, knowing you now have the choice to leave?

This, to me and my girlfriend at least, seemed like a difficult choice. To weigh two worlds against each other, the people in them and what they mean to you. To permanently alter where your home is, and *what* it is. And New Wirral is *different*, too. Yes, the island is small, but the community is tight-knit, everybody has a home and is taken care of! Not to mention the ability to *transform into beasts*. This isn't something you can just forget or disregard, it must leave *some* metaphysical mark on everyone affected by it. Point being, it couldn't have been an easy choice.

"Right guys, it was nice knowing you! Bye bye, see you never!" says the first person. Then the next. One by one, everyone says goodbye, and leaves without hesitation. Then your partner does, and then you do too.

"What?" was the question both my girlfriend and I asked at the same time.

This isn't just an ending that thematically doesn't fit the story and what it was seemingly building up to, it's also just confusing.

*None* of characters even stopped to consider any of what I mentioned above, and the game, unfortunately, doesn't justify any of their decisions beyond "they want to go back!" It doesn't even properly answer *why* they would want to go back.

Kayleigh, our partner of choice, has been on the island for *3 years*, a huge chunk of the life of someone in her 20s, and [does not have a partner](https://wiki.cassettebeasts.com/wiki/Kayleigh/Dialogue) in the world she's from, nor does she ever really mention any friends or family. On the contrary, she's built a new life in New Wirral, is an active part of the community, and now even has a partner there. At some point she mentions that fusing in beast form changed a part of her, as well as that New Wirral changed her appearance to something she feels more comfortable with (or, at least something along the lines of that, as I don't remember the exact line and the wiki is incomplete.) All and all, New Wirral has *definitely* changed her as a person. Yet, despite this, she does not hesitate to jump through the gate to leave this new life behind. Why?

And weigh the complications of actually going back. What if something goes wrong in the portal thingy? What if they land in a different world? And even if they *do* get back to their world, assuming time has passed there as well, it will have been years since anyone's actually seen them - ergo, they've gone missing. Therefore, the implications - funerals have been held, their things given away, whatever they might have had now gone. Hell, their loved ones could have all *died* by this point. They could be coming back to *nothing*. And depending on how everything works back in their worlds, they might struggle to get anything back, too - legally dead persons can't easily get jobs, and it's hard to explain a multiple year gap in the resume! And you certainly can't start yapping on about your adventures transforming into an undead cow, because that will land you in a psych ward.

And speaking of going back to nothing - we know for a *fact* that multiple characters are going back to nothing! Felix, the artist guy, had his apartment transported with him to New Wirral, meaning he did lose at least his home. When he decides to jump back into his world, he's effectively going to be starting from scratch - his possessions gone or destroyed, any essential legal documents he might have had, thus any money he might have had...  Imagine getting your bank account back after being missing for years, with no proof of identity whatsoever. So what is he going back for, exactly..? The only thing he mentions when leaving is that he'll miss New Wirral. Then why is he leaving?

And Meredith, too, mentions being terrible with other people and being all and all fairly alone in her past life. Yet, despite finding new friends (and love, if you decide to date her) in New Wirral, she still decides to up and leave, supposedly to "come back stronger," which barely makes sense. For Meredith, really, the best course of action would have been to start over in New Wirral, but that's not what happens.

Yes, the game does hammer home the point that they are all working towards getting out of New Wirral. However, that does not mean they have to do so at the end, and so casually - quite the opposite. The game, seemingly by accident, sets up the expectation that by the end of it all, at least some of the characters will decide to stay instead of leaving. Why wouldn't they? They keep saying how they want to leave, but also how they have a home and a community, and how they find it comforting to be stuck there with good, decent people. New Wirral seems, although small and flawed, a rather comforting place to be stuck, and realising that home is above "making it in the real world" was what I expected the conclusion to be. It seemed almost so obvious that was the arc the game was going for - only to have that wrecked at the end with little good explanation.

There are some on the internet that say the ending is good, only "bittersweet". But look, no, I'm not criticising it because it's bittersweet. It's not bittersweet. It's confusing, rather poorly thought through, and more than anything, doesn't feel like a logical finish to the plot of the game.

If the goal really was this "everyone leaves ending," that absolutely could have been done. But it should have been better executed. Each character leaving should have been given *good* reasons to go back, good reasons to *hope* that going back is the right choice. But their decisions were not justified, nor thought through - **rather, it feels as though leaving is treated as the *obvious* and inevitable choice anyone would make, but it is.. not.**

I mentioned Oxenfree in this post. I want to compare and contrast this to another ending - that of Oxenfree II. **Spoilers will be in the next paragraph only.**

Oxenfree II utilised a very similar premise at the end of the game, and, forgive me, but yes, I am pointing this out because it is very similar visually too - a big triangle gate into the abyss, and you are given a choice. In short, the gate must be closed, but to be closed, *someone* must go inside and stay in a different world forever. That world is fake - it is merely a phantom representation of the real word, but it lures you, for in it, you might find happiness. Perhaps temporarily, perhaps forever. And perhaps if it's temporary, it will eventually turn into pain. Nevertheless, you cannot truly know, unless you step in. But if you do decide to you step in, you are abandoning this world for eternity, and you can *never* come back. And in that sense, this decision, well, it is quite similar, certainly in its gravity.

Oxenfree makes it clear that this is not an obvious choice to make. Because you *cannot* know for a fact what the right choice to make is. This is in contrast to Cassette Beasts, which almost unapologetically decides that leaving is the right choice, the *only* choice, even if there is next to nothing to substantiate *why*, except, perhaps, that the "real world" is more important than the "fake" one, i.e. New Wirral. Which, looked at from that perspective, is even more frustrating, as it's the game effectively devaluing its own world in favour of the mess we have in the real world, despite the fact that, really, New Wirral is just as real as the other universes; it being an anomaly does not take away any of its "realness" to the people that inhabit it. And this all is what bugs me.

But there is *one* more thing that bugs me. I mentioned Oxenfree, but I also mentioned the metaphysical, not for the sake of using big words in my pointless essay that no one will read, I mentioned it because the entire game revolves around the metaphysical. Unfortunately not in a very detailed sense, which disappoints me a bit, but it still revolves around it. The game is entirely about *transforming* into different beings, and also introduces a rather amazing mechanic of *fusing* with another person temporarily.

Really, in practice, the effect it would actually have on the psyches of people on New Wirral, I don't think it could be as easily ignored as the game makes it seem. If you live a good chunk of your life as *a human being*, your identity set in stone as just a person, how can you just walk away from something as life and identity altering as becoming something else at will? And even more deeply, how can you walk away from the fact you have on occasion literally *become one* with another person? Kayleigh mentions that fusing has had a strange effect on her and her identity, and how she feels about herself and you, her partner. Yet, somehow, rather confusingly, that effect is not strong enough to play a role in her decision to leave New Wirral.

There is one fair point you can make though - if practically losing your body, mind and sense of self in the process of fusing with another person doesn't affect how you feel about yourself enough to change you over the course of 3 years, nothing probably ever will. What is a character arc, anyway?

The dog follows you at least.
