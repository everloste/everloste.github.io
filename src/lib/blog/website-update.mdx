my website's felt stale for quite a bit now.
well, it's a good thing i was procrastinating on other things,
because i found the time to make some upgrades.

of course, those tiny upgrades turned into an entire website remodel,
but i'm glad for it.

here's a little blog post on how it went.

## the near-infinite world of potential designs

the main challenge i encountered was deciding what design or feeling i wanted my website to have. there were multiple ideas in my head i really liked, but if i wanted a polished, unitary design, i couldn't combine them.

the previous design was intended to be modern (if not a tinyy bit 'futuristic', at least in terms of vibes) in combination with the classic look of computing consoles, and it kind of worked. but it didn't quite do its job, and it felt unfinished.

so, for a new design, these were the options i considered:

**#1: keep the previous idea and redo it.**
a somewhat scifi-esque, retrocomputing-styled, perhaps spacey kind of design.
similar to the previous one, but a bit more focused.
the only problem is that, a lot of what makes retro consoles feel the way they do, is the way you interact with them - with keyboard only.
there's no world in which that would work for a website, so right from the start
we are making significant compromises. not a good start.
to top it off, frankly, it feels too limiting.
a design like that could be great if executed well,
but it would encapsulate what is ultimately a representation of me, my space,
in a very inflexible manner.

**#2: build it around some cosy pixel art.** most, if not all, visual work i do you can call 'pixel art'.
there were a couple of issues with this, however.
first, i primarily do texturing and 3d modelling, not typical drawing,
which limits what kind of art i can put up.
second - the same problem as before - it would feel personally kind of limiting.
it would put me, my space, in too small and narrow a box.
it might make everything i do feel like is somehow connected to pixel art work. but it's not.

**#3: genericise it a bit,** and build the website around what i like *in a website*, as opposed to in general, creative work-wise.
it might not end up being entirely original and unique,
but it will work the best in practice,
while not sorting me or my work in a small box.
it will allow me to be flexible.

## making space
i intend to make this website a home for more of the stuff i do.
as such, i needed to tidy some corners,
and slightly redesign the website for other content to fit in as well.

there's currently nothing here, but one of the first things i would like to do
is start publishing some of my own creative writing.
it may or may not be a good idea to do this way,
but i want it to live somewhere that's mine, so this website it is.

in order to make space for it, i redesigned the blog page (which is now called 'writing'),
and added tags so you can easily differentiate between types of posts.
i will add a filter later too, but it's currently not necessary.

the *minecraft projects* page has disappeared from the navigation bar as well.
the link to it now lives on the projects page.
the reason for this is simply the fact that i do all kinds of things,
and differentiating between them in the navigation bar is impractical.
from now on, everything will live on the projects page.

lastly, i added the *support me* and *contact me* pages.
these are self-explanatory!
why they've only been added now, i'm not sure,
but it's very useful to have these.

for the *contact me* page i took inspiration from [Tom Scott's website](https://www.tomscott.com/),
because i quite like the way he's designed it!
it should hopefully make it clear not just where to contact me -
as my communication channels are, at the moment, a huge mess -
but also clarify when you should or shouldn't contact me.
despite the fact my presence on the internet is nowhere near the size of Tom Scott,
and likely never will be,
i still get many unsolicited messages about things that frankly don't need to be answered directly, and privately, by me.
hopefully it at least clarifies why i often don't respond.

## conclusion
hopefully you like the new design. i think i do.

i have done quite a bit of 'research' on personal sites during this,
trying to see if there's anything i could take inspiration from.
i'm not sure i learned anything, though.
most people now just use pre-made templates of different variations,
which all start looking the same after a while.
it's kind of depressing.

those that don't often just use simple HTML and CSS to make
rather early 2010s-esque webpages.
i am very fond of that, because it shows effort and creativity that
wasn't put into websites made with templates.
but i can't say i could learn much from them, either.

and anyway, it's not just templates, but the general style too.
for example,
a lot of websites now seem to share the Google-inspired rounded, blobby,
plainly-coloured design, and while i think it can look rather good,
it really does get stale quickly.
there are more examples of this - such as the vibecoding favourite, "monospaced font and gradients" - 
but you know, you get the idea.

i don't really know what the conclusion to take away from this is,
but i'm happy my website is unique in this sense.
it doesn't need to perfect or completely original,
i'm just proud to say it's almost completely handmade.

i enjoyed making it, hopefully you can enjoy browsing it.
